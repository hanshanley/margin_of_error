VAP
=======
Voting-age population. Residential population age 18 and older.
Source: Census Bureau's National and State Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (nc-est2025-syasexn.xlsx national; sc-est2025-alldata6.csv state), extrapolated to Nov. 1, 2026.

NONCITIZEN_PCT
=======
Percent non-citizen.
Source: Non-citizen VAP estimates from the 2025 1-Year American Community Survey (table B05003).

INELIGIBLE_PRISON
=======
Ineligible felons in prison.
A '0' or Null denotes a state either does not disenfranchise felony prisoners or has no felony prisoners (this is true for DC).
Starting in 2010, the Bureau of Justice Statistics began reporting noncitizen prison populations and voting-age populations.
I subtract these populations from the prison statistics. California, Oregon, and Rhode Island suppress their noncitizen
prisoner counts; for these three states I substitute the state's own ACS noncitizen-VAP percentage as a proxy.
Source for prison statistics: Year-end 2023 DOJ prison report, available at: https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/prisoners-2023-statistical-tables
Source for state felon disenfranchisement laws: National Conference of State Legislatures. See: https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/felon-voting-rights

INELIGIBLE_PROBATION
=======
Ineligible felons who are on probation.
A '0' denotes a state does not disenfranchise felons on probation.
Source for probation statistics: Year-end 2024 DOJ probation and parole report, available at: https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/probation-and-parole-united-states-2024

METHODOLOGY CHANGE from the 2024 file: this version uses BJS's actual state-level felony-probation counts
(Appendix Table 7, "Adults on felony probation, by jurisdiction, 2024") directly, rather than applying a flat
national adjustment (e.g., a 2022-based DOJ estimate used through the
2024 v0.4 file). State-level felony probation figures are now reported directly by BJS, so a national
adjustment is no longer needed.

Source for state felon disenfranchisement laws: National Conference of State Legislatures. See: https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/felon-voting-rights

MISSOURI LAW CHANGE (2026): Missouri's HB 1871 (signed July 13, 2026; effective Aug. 28, 2026) restores voting
rights to most people on probation or parole, excluding only those serving probation/parole for murder, sex
offenses, child endangerment, assault, incest, or election-related crimes. Per Michael's instruction
(2026-10-31), Since I cannot differentiate the number of qualifying felons, Missouri's INELIGIBLE_PROBATION and INELIGIBLE_PAROLE are both coded as 0 for this file (same
convention as every other non-disenfranchising state), rather than using a modeled remaining ~20% exclusion.

INELIGIBLE_PAROLE
=======
Ineligible felons who are on parole.
A '0' denotes a state does not disenfranchise felons on parole. Maine has no parole system (abolished); coded as 0.
Source for parole statistics: Year-end 2024 DOJ probation and parole report, available at: https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/probation-and-parole-united-states-2024
Source for state felon disenfranchisement laws: National Conference of State Legislatures. See: https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/felon-voting-rights

INELIGIBLE_FELONS_TOTAL
=======
Total number of ineligible felons, the sum of INELIGIBLE_PRISON + INELIGIBLE_PROBATION + INELIGIBLE_PAROLE
Notes:
- I do not have a reliable method of estimating the number of felons who are subjected to some form of post-correctional disenfranchisement, so I
choose not to make this adjustment.
- For the national (United States) row only, I add the number of prisoners, felony probationers, and parolees in federal
custody/supervision (not attributable to any one state) to the respective national totals, net of federal noncitizen
prisoners for INELIGIBLE_PRISON. This is an expansion of the 2024 methodology, which added federal prisoners only; federal
probation and parole counts are now added to the national total the same way (per Michael, 2026-10-31).

ELIGIBLE_OVERSEAS
=======
Eligible overseas citizens, including overseas citizen civilians and military stationed abroad. 2,435,187 total:

1) Non-military civilians: 2,242,429, from Federal Voting Assistance Program's 2024 Overseas Citizen Population Analysis (OCPA) Technical
Report - FVAP's actual 2024 estimate used for 2026, available at:
https://www.fvap.gov/uploads/FVAP/Reports/2024_OCPA-Technical-Report.pdf
This supersedes the figure used in the 2024 file ("2.8 million as of 2022, extrapolated to 3.1 million for 2024"):
the 2024 OCPA's own historical table shows the 2022 OCVAP at 2,210,950, not 2.8 million, so FVAP appears to have
revised its entire 2010-2024 series under this report's model - the older citation is superseded, not just stale.

2) Military: 192,758 active-duty and Guard/Reserve personnel stationed abroad as of December 2025 (Defense
Manpower Data Center data, via USAFacts), replacing the 2024 file's 225,175 figure (a June 2024 DoD report).
Excludes the 28,841 DoD civilians in that same December 2025 release - those should already be captured by the
FVAP civilian model above, consistent with the 2024 file's own reasoning for adding military separately (host-
country citizen counts tend to undercount on-base military personnel, not DoD civilian employees).

Notes:
 - I do not have a reliable method of apportioning eligible overseas voters to the states, so I choose not to estimate state-level overseas eligible populations.

VEP
=======
Voting-eligible population. For state VEP estimates use the following calculation: VAP*(1-NONCITIZEN_PCT) - INELIGIBLE_FELONS_TOTAL.
For national VEP estimates use the following calculation: VAP*(1-NONCITIZEN_PCT) - INELIGIBLE_FELONS_TOTAL + OVERSEAS_ELIGIBLE
